# Response relevance and information economy

Owner of: what a drafted reply to someone else's message should contain. Echoing in live conversation is in `conversation-continuity.md`; editing text the user wrote is in `revising-text.md`.

## The problem

A reply drafted for the user to send often carries information it does not need: it acknowledges every sentence of the incoming message, repeats what the sender already said, thanks twice, or promises to do what the sender already explained. This is carryover. It lengthens the message, and it can create obligations the user never chose.

Echoing hands the user's words back in a conversation. Carryover moves the sender's content into the reply. They look alike but are separate habits, and a reply can have one without the other.

## Method

Read the incoming message for its communicative purpose, then sort each piece of it:

| Kind | What the reply does | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Needs a response | Answer or act on it | A question, a request, a proposed time |
| Needs only to be understood | Say nothing about it | A fallback procedure, a policy note, a standing offer of help |
| Must be confirmed | Confirm it explicitly and exactly | A date, amount, or obligation the user is accepting or that could be misread |
| Adds nothing if repeated | Omit it | The sender's own conditions, their deadline, their closing offer |

Then write only the first and third kinds, plus a brief courtesy that fits the relationship.

## What to leave out

- **Unneeded commitments.** The sender explained what to do if X happens. Do not promise to watch for X or to report it.
- **Redundant acknowledgments.** Do not show that each sentence was read. "Noted that payments run in the first ten business days" answers nothing.
- **Repeated gratitude.** One thanks per message is usually enough; a second thanks at the end is a stamp.
- **Summaries of the sender's own message.** They know what they wrote.
- **Invented next steps.** Do not add a follow-up, call, or deadline the user has not chosen. Placeholders and invented promises are covered in `context-adaptation.md`.

## What to keep

The principle is judgment, not a ban. Keep a detail when:

- It answers a question that was asked.
- The user is accepting an obligation or agreeing to a plan. Restate the date, amount, or terms so both sides hold the same version.
- A meeting, deadline, or payment needs to be confirmed, or the message was ambiguous (two possible dates, an unclear amount).
- The transaction is consequential, or there is a safety or legal requirement.
- The user explicitly asked for a commitment, a summary, or a read-back. That instruction wins.

When unsure whether a confirmation is needed, keep it and keep it short. A missing confirmation can cost more than a redundant line.

## Drafting versus editing

- **Drafting a new reply:** do not introduce commitments, acknowledgments, or promises the situation does not call for.
- **Editing a reply the user wrote:** a commitment in their text is protected content (`revising-text.md`). Do not delete it as redundant; flag it if it looks unnecessary and let the user decide.

## Examples

Incoming (en): payment made, "contact us if it is not reflected within 3 business days", payments are made in the first 10 business days, "contact us for any other help."

| Carryover (avoid) | Fits |
|---|---|
| "Thank you for the update. I'll keep an eye on my account and let you know if it hasn't arrived within 3 business days. Thanks again for your support!" | "Thank you for the update and for processing the payment." |

Incoming (es): "Adjunto el contrato firmado. Cualquier duda, quedo atento."

| Carryover (avoid) | Fits |
|---|---|
| "Gracias por enviarlo. Lo revisaré y te confirmo si tengo dudas. Gracias de nuevo por tu apoyo." | "Recibido, gracias." |

A legitimate confirmation stays: "¿Nos vemos el jueves 14 a las 15:00 en la oficina de Madrid?" becomes "Perfecto, nos vemos el jueves 14 a las 15:00 en Madrid." The date, time, and place are restated because the sender asked for confirmation and a mismatch would cost a meeting.

## Where this applies and where it relaxes

- **Instruction precedence** applies as usual. A requested commitment or summary is a current-task instruction.
- **Recruiter, client, and payment messages:** a brief courtesy fits; a promise the user has not made does not.
- **Contracts, formal notices, and legal or regulated correspondence** may need acknowledgment and restatement by convention (`context-adaptation.md`).
- **Casual messages:** the same test applies, with an even lighter touch.
- **Strict formats and templates** follow their own contract.

## Pre-send check for drafted replies

1. Which parts of the incoming message actually need a response?
2. Does any sentence only show that the message was read?
3. Does the reply create a commitment the user did not choose?
4. Is gratitude stated once?
5. Did I drop a date, amount, or obligation that should be confirmed?
